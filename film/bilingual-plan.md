# Bilingual design — Hebrew film that also deepens Lavi's French

Lavi already speaks French well. This is not beginner teaching: no slowed syllables, no
"répète après moi" drills. It is a naming game at natural speed, anchored to what is on screen at
that exact moment.

Michel speaks both languages himself — his cloned voice carries French convincingly, confirmed by
test. No separate French voice is needed.

## The recurring shape

Michel points at something real in frame, asks in French, leaves a beat for Lavi to answer, then
answers and adds one short sentence that carries new information.

> **מישל:** "לביא, תראה. **Comment ça s'appelle ?**"
> *beat — Lavi answers at home*
> "**Un hélicoptère. Il vole très haut, et il aide les policiers.**"

Approved level. Concrete nouns, one simple added fact, spoken at normal speed.

## The four moments

| Sequence | Object | French |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — helicopter | מסוק | Comment ça s'appelle ? — Un hélicoptère. Il vole très haut, et il aide les policiers. |
| 2 — theft | שוקולד | Où est le chocolat ? — the question Michel is literally asking in the plot |
| 3 — police car | ניידת / מכשיר קשר | C'est une voiture de police. Elle a des lumières bleues. |
| 5 — ending | שוטר | Le policier aide les gens. |

Two moments reuse **C'est**, and the helicopter and car moments share the naming-question frame.
That repetition is the teaching.

### The joke that writes itself

The thief, caught with the Kinder, says "סלט."
Michel corrects him, completely deadpan: **"Non. Du chocolat."**

## Production note

French moments must show Michel's face and the object he points at — they cannot be VO over
B-roll, because a child learning a word needs to see the mouth and the thing at once. Where the
cut plan converted an educational wrap to VO, the French moment stays on camera instead.
